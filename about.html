Method

The text

All quotations are from Stanisław Lem, Summa Technologiae, translated by Joanna Zylinska (University of Minnesota Press, 2013, Electronic Mediations 40), cited by page. That edition carries chapters 1–8; the closing chapter, Sztuka i technologia, and Lem's 1974 afterword are cited from the Polish where used. Every quotation in the book is checked mechanically against the text (verify.sh).

Two layers

Lem refused to be graded on devices. So each essay scores him twice: at the object level — did the thing he described come to exist? — and at the problem level — did the dilemma he attached to it become the live one? A chapter can win one layer and lose the other, and several do.

Seven verdicts

  • Hit — described it; it happened substantially as described.
  • Miss — described it; it did not, and there is no live path.
  • Early — not yet, but the path is live, and we say what the date depends on.
  • Right question, wrong answer — the dilemma is the live one; his resolution is not.
  • Right answer, wrong reason — the outcome matches; the mechanism doesn't.
  • Dissolved — the premise fell and the question no longer means anything.
  • Open — 2026 cannot say, and the essay names what evidence would settle it.

Every ledger row is one specific claim of Lem's, paged, against one specific fact about the present, sourced. Claims about the present are the ones a reader will actually dispute, so each carries a source and a check marker; the script confirms the marker has a source, and a human confirms the source says what we say.

What this cannot prove

That our reading of Lem is fair, or that our account of 2026 is right. Both are checked by reading — ours, a reviewer panel's, and yours. If a verdict is wrong, open an issue.
