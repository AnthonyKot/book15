Sixty Years Later

In 1964 Stanisław Lem published Summa Technologiae, a book about the problems a technological civilization would be forced to face — machine intelligence, artificial realities, the redesign of its own biology, the silence of the sky. He asked to be judged on the problems, not the gadgets. Sixty-two years on, this is that judgement: ten essays, one per chapter, each ending in a ledger of specific claims against specific facts, scored with a vocabulary that has more than two words in it.
