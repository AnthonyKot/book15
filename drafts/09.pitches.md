# Essay 9 — replacing ch. 9 — pitches

**Contract amendment required first.** §6 assigns essay 9 to ch. 9 "Art and Technology", which
is absent from the 2013 translation. The user has agreed to replace it. These pitches draw on
two secondary sources supplied 2026-08-18, both now in `resources/`:

- Peter Swirski, "The Cassandra Syndrome, or How Not to Be a Prophet", *Filozofia i Nauka* 10
  (2022) — Lem's translator and interviewer arguing Lem should *not* be read as a futurologist.
- Peter Butko, "Summa technologiae – Looking Back and Ahead", in Swirski (ed.), *The Art and
  Science of Stanislaw Lem* (McGill-Queen's, 2006), pp. 81–103 — a biophysicist's chapter-length
  assessment, and the source Zylinska quotes on Summa as "a work in progress".

Load-bearing material:

- Swirski's verdict: "Should Lem be read, not to say revered, as a futurologist? On balance
  evidence suggests that in any proper—which is to say scientific—understanding of the term, the
  answer has to be No." And from his 1992 interviews: "more than in predicting the future he has
  always been more interested in mapping the conceptual limits of science and its technological
  offshoots."
- The unfalsifiability charge, demonstrated with his own parody forecast: open-ended claims where
  "Heads I win, tails you lose."
- Lem's self-assessments, per Swirski, in *The Mystery of the Chinese Room* (1996), *The Megabit
  Bomb* (1999) and *The Blink of an Eye* (2000): among his claimed successes are "the adaptive
  morphology of the human brain, biocomputing, and autonomous search engines (Lem refers to the
  latter as ariadnology: the science of threads)". Also punctuated equilibrium "a full eight
  years before it entered the contemporary evolutionary lexicon via Eldredge and Gould".
- Swirski's charge that "he is not above cherry-picking his fiction for predictive hits", and his
  note that fans "keep score cards, with check marks next to the fulfilled prognoses and question
  marks next to the perceived flops" — i.e. this book is a genre, and he thinks it misconceived.
- Butko (2006) as a second scorecard, twenty years before this one.

## 9-A · "The Cassandra syndrome"

Take the strongest case against this book and test it against the book's own ledgers. Swirski
says Lem is not a futurologist; the layer split says he is right — 22% plain HITs on devices and
dates against 43% on problems, and no MISSes at problem level at all. But the book also refutes
half his argument: the "heads I win, tails you lose" charge fails for the object-level rows,
which are specific enough that four of them were falsified outright. And the two scorecards
collide on one concrete point — Lem claimed ariadnology as a hit for search engines, while essay
7 scored his machine-librarian claim a MISS, because he called frequency analysis worthless and
counting citations became PageRank. The closing argument of the book, and the only essay that
can use the other nine as evidence.

## 9-B · "The author's scorecard"

Grade the self-grading. Lem returned to Summa three times to mark his own work, and Swirski
lists what he claimed: adaptive morphology of the brain, biocomputing, autonomous search
engines, punctuated equilibrium before Eldredge and Gould. Run those claims through this book's
rules and see how many survive — essay 0 scored OPEN on whether an outside verdict beats the
author's own precisely because this comparison could not be made. Risk: the self-assessments are
in Polish collections not held here, so every claim is filtered through Swirski's summary, and
the essay would be grading a report of a grading.

## 9-C · "Two scorecards, twenty years apart"

Butko assessed Summa's science in 2006 as a biophysicist, before the English translation
existed; this book assessed it in 2026 with the translation and sixty years of receipts. Put the
two side by side: what looked fulfilled in 2006 and looks open now, what looked open in 2006 and
has closed. The interesting cases are biological — autoevolution, information farming — where
Butko's field is the one that moved. Risk: needs close reading of a 23-page chapter, and the
comparison may be thinner than it sounds.

## 9-D · "The missing chapter"

Write the absence. Chapter 9 is the book's only chapter about art, it is not in this edition,
and essay 0 already notes that DISSOLVED went unused on a corpus missing exactly that chapter.
Ask what the book cannot say: whether Lem's questions survive in the one domain where 2026 has
changed most visibly and where his framework — technology as a means to goals nobody chose —
would be most tested. Risk: an essay about what is not there, with no corpus to gate it.
