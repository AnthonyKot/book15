# Essay 8 — ledger draft (angle 8-A, "The invisible path")

Dating caveat: the 2013 translation is of a later edition; this chapter's conclusion is
signed "Krakow, August 1966", so the matchmaking passage is mid-1960s, not 1964. The
baseline is set accordingly.

## Object level

| Lem (page) | 2026 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Machine-mediated pairing will be widely adopted and become "a cultural norm" (pp. 344–345) | Meeting online became the most common way US heterosexual couples meet, passing friends around 2013 | HIT |
| Two-level selection: match psychosomatic profiles, then reject genotypes likely to produce offspring "considered undesirable… disabled…, or endowed with a low level of intelligence, or with unstable personalities" (p. 345) | The selector exists and sells that exact menu — IQ, anxiety, ADHD, appearance, disease risk — but it screens embryos, not partners | RIGHT ANSWER, WRONG REASON |
| "It would be relatively easy, for example, to aim to distribute widely high intelligence" (p. 346) | Not easy, and not by selection: choosing the top of ten embryos on a cognitive polygenic score buys ~2.5 IQ points with wide intervals, bounded by within-family variance | MISS |
| Cyborgization rejected: no gut removal, just "microattachments… to introduce certain substances into his system" (p. 343) | Implanted pumps and hormone implants are routine; no space programme has proposed removing organs | HIT |
| Average life span past 100 "without interfering with man's genetic information, seems unrealistic to me" (p. 340) | No population is close; gains have decelerated since 1990 and radical extension is judged implausible this century | HIT |

## Problem level

| Lem (page) | 2026 | Verdict |

## Where he was better / worse

Better: he located the moral problem in the asymmetry of disgust, and in the information
problem (a plan nobody can evaluate is not consented to). Worse: he assumed the unit of
selection would stay the couple, so he assumed a millennial timescale; IVF moved the unit
to the embryo and removed the speed limit — while polygenic architecture put a different,
harder limit in its place that he could not have seen.
