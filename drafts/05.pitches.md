# Essay 5 — Prolegomena to Omnipotence (pp. 164–196) — pitches

Chapter sections: Before Chaos (164) · Chaos and Order (168) · Scylla and Charybdis: On
Restraint (172) · The Silence of the Designer (176) · Methodological Madness (179) ·
A New Linnaeus (184) · Models and Reality (186) · Plagiarism and Creation (190) ·
On Imitology (193). Contract (CONTEXT §6) also hands this essay ch. 4's "The Beliefs of
Electric Brains" (pp. 124–141).

Load-bearing passages found:

- p. 176–179 **the Designer**: refuses "definitive" questions; "properties belong to
  situations, not things"; "'real' means the same as 'useful' to him"; everything is to be
  settled by experiment, and if no experiment is possible "the issue disappears for him".
- p. 179–181 **the mad tailor**: mathematics sews clothes for nobody; Russell — "the subject
  in which we never know what we are talking about"; physicists have been "searching through
  the warehouses of 'empty clothes'" since the sixteenth century; matrix calculus was empty
  until Heisenberg. p. 182–183 math as **ladder, not image**: "we must not ask whether the
  ladder is 'true'".
- p. 183 the reversal: mathematics "can start giving orders to physics… a phantomological
  generator, a creator of worlds".
- p. 186–188 **Models and Reality**: modelling must ignore variables; adding variables makes
  the model diverge in other respects; "The most perfect model of an apple is offered by
  another apple, of the Universe by another Universe" (p. 188) — and he calls this a reductio
  of imitological practice, then refuses to accept it, because a *theory* holds more
  information than the phenomenon. p. 188–189 the **information balance** question: where did
  the extra information come from? "a true informational perpetuum mobile!"
- p. 190–192 **Plagiarism and Creation**: don't repeat Nature's road, enter "sideways" —
  synthesise penicillin instead of growing mould; write the chromosome down "on a piece of
  paper, in the symbolic language of chemistry" and synthesise it; then "talent matrices".
- p. 193–196 **On Imitology**: everything man does is modelling; design as an "amplifier of
  the states of low probability"; Nature's probability distribution vs ours.
- p. 172–176 **restraint**: navigate between Scylla (the unsettled foundations of physics)
  and Charybdis (cybernetic garrulousness); "restraint on the level of design".

## 5-A · "Real means useful"

The Designer's pragmatism (pp. 176–179) is the actual working epistemology of machine
learning in 2026: ship what benchmarks say works, decline the definitive question, treat
"why does it generalise" as a question that disappears if no experiment settles it. Test:
scaling as an empirical law with no accepted theory; interpretability as the debt the field
keeps rolling over; the reproducibility and benchmark-contamination arguments as the price.
Where he is better than us: he says out loud that this is a *choice about which questions to
drop*, and names what it costs. Likely misses: he thought the designer would still be a
person; the restraint he prescribes is a virtue nobody has time for.

## 5-B · "The universal plagiarizer"

p. 188's line — the perfect model of an apple is another apple — and its companion in ch. 7
(p. 241, "a machine that would copy, with utmost exactitude, every material phenomenon would
be a universal plagiarizer… cut off from any creative activity") read straight into the 2026
argument about whether a model trained on everything compresses or merely copies. Test:
memorisation-vs-generalisation studies, model collapse on synthetic data, the "stochastic
parrot" argument and its rebuttals, and the fact that the *lossy* model is the useful one.
Also his information-perpetuum-mobile question (p. 189): does a theory contain more than was
put in? — against self-play (AlphaZero) and synthetic-data bootstrapping. Risk: this is the
essay with the most 2026 discourse in it, so the ledger must be strict about what is settled.

## 5-C · "The mad tailor's warehouse"

Mathematics as empty clothes (pp. 179–183), physics rummaging for a fit — and Lem's forecast
that the traffic will one day reverse, mathematics ordering physics about (p. 183). Test both
directions: deep learning ran on clothes sewn long before (matrix calculus — his own example
— convolution, gradient descent), while the field still has no theory of why the clothes fit;
and machines have now entered the warehouse themselves (automated theorem proving,
formalisation, discovered algorithms). Where he was better: "the ladder is not the mountain"
is a cleaner statement of the modelling relation than most 2026 talk about world models.

## 5-D · "The side entrance"

Plagiarism and Creation (pp. 190–192): don't retrace Nature's path, arrive at its endpoint
from somewhere else — synthesised penicillin, chromosomes written out in chemical notation
and rebuilt, "talent matrices". Test: synthetic genomes, directed evolution, structure
prediction that skips the folding physics, and the embryo-screening industry as the ugly
literal version of the talent matrix. This is the essay where his optimism and his blind spot
sit in the same paragraph. Overlap risk: autoevolution is essay 8's job — this must stay on
*method* (side entrance vs. plagiarism), not on the ethics of editing people.
