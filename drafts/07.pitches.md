## 7-A · "Predictions stripped of explanations"

His sharpest, most testable forecast in the whole book (pp. 276–277): control without
understanding, science that predicts and cannot explain. Test: structure prediction that
works without solving the folding problem, learned weather models beating numerical ones,
materials discovered by search rather than theory, and the argument inside science about
whether that counts as knowledge. The ledger's job is to check the *bargain* he described —
did science accept it, and did the "comprehending language" survive as he said it would, as
the place where value judgements live?

## 7-B · "Information farming"

The engineering proposal itself: grow theories the way an egg grows an organism, generation
after generation through sieves. Test against what actually automated: not growth but
search plus selection, exactly Ashby's half of the argument that Lem said he was departing
from. Automated hypothesis generation, robot labs, the "AI scientist" papers, and the
question his design raises and ours has not answered — who does the *selecting*, and against
what environment. His combinatorial-wall numbers (p. 239) are checkable and probably the
cleanest dated MISS available: he ruled out what brute force later did.

## 7-C · "The language that acts"

Linguistic engineering (pp. 267–281) is the chapter that partly answers the omission essay 4
records: Lem *does* treat language as a substrate — the causal language of heredity — and
predicts a third language, built outside brains, that crosses into comprehension by accident.
Test: models grown from natural language that now act causally through tools and agents; and
the literal causal-language engineering of DNA and protein language models. Also his
gap-filling account of natural language (p. 269) against prompting, and his claim that
formal languages leave no gaps against the fact that ours are ambiguous by design.

## 7-D · "The nested worlds"

Cosmogonic engineering: the constructor's problem is not building the world but hiding the
exit (pp. 289–291), and the inhabitants' physicist who discovers he is made of impulses and
changes nothing. Test: the simulation argument as it stands in 2026, generative world models
built to train agents inside, and — for the transcendence half — the grief-tech industry
selling the dead back to the living, against his conclusion that engineered transcendence
changes nothing about the life before it. Coordination: essay 6 already scored the
*detection* side (can you tell?); this essay takes the *constructor* side only.
