### 1. The only `MISS` grades a hedged example the next paragraph walks back, and pays it with a truncated bundle

**Location:** ledger, first object row — “Reversed. All sixteen nuclear chromosomes of yeast synthesised… no orchestration of traits in fetuses”; “Where he was worst”: “He got the order of difficulty backwards.”

**Problem:** The Lem sentence is: a better theory will let us “control evolution, change the speed and range of an organism’s regenerative potential, or orchestrate genetic traits in fetuses. All this will turn out to be possible long before, *say*, we gain the ability to create the chromosome apparatus of a nucleus in an artificial manner” (p. 193). Two sentences later in the same section he writes that “one should not take on an additional risk by predicting any future development of individual sciences” (p. 193). The chapter’s own method note, unquoted, is “We shall never say that things will be a certain way, only that they can be a certain way” (p. 185).

The 2026 cell then collapses the first bundle to the one item that failed (fetal orchestration) and treats Sc2.0’s sixteen separately synthesised-and-characterised yeast chromosomes as the nuclear apparatus. That is the same truncation family as the retired Gibson `HIT`. As of the 2023 Cell special issue the consortium had all sixteen chromosomes built and a strain with *over half* a synthetic genome; they had not put the apparatus of one nucleus into one cell. Directed evolution (Arnold) and CRISPR are live forms of “control evolution”; they arrived before, not after, those chromosomes. He Jiankui (2018) is a crude, failed attempt to write a trait into human embryos and is not mentioned. A 2025 society *call* for a moratorium is evidence the germline path is live enough to brake, which is the opposite of §4 `MISS` (“no live path”).

`MISS` on “A long before B” is not earned when A is a three-item “or” list partly realised, B is incomplete, the adverb is “say,” and the next paragraph declines to predict individual sciences.

**Fix:** Drop the row, or narrow it to the one comparison that can be scored (fetal-trait *orchestration* vs a finished synthetic nucleus) and mark it `OPEN` or `EARLY`. If a negative is needed, use a claim he did not walk back (finding 3).

### 2. `RIGHT ANSWER, WRONG REASON` on p. 125 is an analogy the prose already refuses

**Location:** problem-level row — “Language models answer rather than abstain — but because the grading rewards a guess, not because refusal would end them. A parallel, not the same mechanism” / `RIGHT ANSWER, WRONG REASON`. Happened: “reaches a formally similar place by another road.”

**Problem:** pp. 124–125: a homeostat whose significant variables, if transgressed, *threaten its existence* must treat incomplete information as complete, because “Refraining from action would mean stopping life processes.” Kalai et al. 2025 describe exam-style grading that rewards a guess over abstention. An LLM is not that homeostat. Guessing under uncertainty is true of any decision system; it is not Lem’s distinctive claim. §4 `RIGHT ANSWER, WRONG REASON` requires that the *outcome* match. The outcome that matches is a tautology. The essay’s own 2026 cell says the mechanisms are not the same, then awards the answer anyway. This is the leftover the last panel already named.

**Fix:** Delete the row, or keep the comparison as a labelled analogy with no verdict. A problem-level row on forced induction needs a regulator that actually dies (or is taken offline) if it refuses to act.

### 3. After finding 1, the chapter’s honest negatives are the ones not in the ledger

**Location:** “Models and Reality” / p. 188, unquoted; talent matrices, p. 192, in prose only as “not gradeable either way.”

**Problem:** AGENT requires a real `MISS` or `RIGHT QUESTION, WRONG ANSWER`. The present `MISS` is finding 1. The present `RIGHT ANSWER, WRONG REASON` is finding 2. What the chapter actually gets wrong is left ungraded:

- **p. 188, own voice:** “as long as the number of variables is small, digital modeling works well. On increasing their number, this method quickly reaches the limit of its applicability. The modeling approach therefore has to be replaced by a different one.” Climate models, molecular dynamics, and learned models scaled digital methods instead of replacing them. Prior panels were right that *parameters ≠ Lem’s selected variables* and that the surrounding sentence is about modeling *all* variables. That is a reason to write the row carefully, not a reason to omit the only “this method has to be replaced” sentence in the chapter.
- **p. 192 talent matrices:** he treats comprehensive knowledge of “the genetic codes that enact particular talents” as what unlocks embedding musical or mathematical talent in any egg. The live 2026 practice is selection among embryos already made (`embryo-screening-2024`), not a code-table write. “Where he was worst” already names this as the same shape of error; the ledger leaves it out because the antecedent is unmet. Unmet antecedent is `OPEN` for the *device*. The *mechanism* (cipher ⇒ trait) is `RIGHT QUESTION, WRONG ANSWER`.

**Fix:** Put one of these in the ledger, receipted, as the contract negative. Do not leave the negative only in a paragraph the scores do not support.

### 4. The ladder is still graded, just off-ledger

Lem says the ladder can give height and slope, and that a theory may be “true as a whole”; he denies a required part-by-part physical double. The reader still receives a falsification of p. 183, paid by receipts that do not touch his claim, with no verdict they can contest. “Lem’s own Designer would have wanted this” is the essay speaking for him.

**Fix:** Keep the analogy if it teaches, and stop declaring the rule broken. Cut the lede’s “question he ruled meaningless.” Othello/SAE may illustrate interpretability; they do not score Lem.

### 5. Omitted sentences that change the surplus-information paragraph

**Problem:** The immediately preceding lines (p. 188) *do* answer it at the level of the world: “It came from the fact that, generally speaking, there exists a continuity of transformations in the world. The essay’s “cannot answer” flattens a provisional physical answer plus an open accounting problem into a shrug. The `OPEN` row on the balance question is the right verdict; the said paragraph is not the right report of the argument.

**Fix:** Quote the continuity/feedback sentence, then the `x + n` problem. Score only the accounting question.

### 7. Still-open #3 is the essay’s question, and one closer would not close it

AGENT: the still-open section is not a dumping ground. “A decade of capability claims nobody can check” would show prolonged bad measurement; it would not show that uncontaminated usefulness cannot be measured.

**Fix:** Replace with a Lem question the ledger left `OPEN` (p. 184 generator; p. 188–189 surplus, with a closer that is actually an accounting), or drop to two items. Closure evidence: a regime that survives optimization, or a demonstration that every proposed regime fails under it.
