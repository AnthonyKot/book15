### 1. The cost MISS prices the wrong thing, then the essay uses that cheapness to unsay the MISS

**Location:** ledger object row, “This kind of investment would have to be even more substantial than the current investment in research on nuclear energy” → **MISS**; “What happened”: “A serious search never cost anything like nuclear-energy research and never needed to”; “Where he was worst”: “By his own condition, we are not yet entitled to the conclusion the chapter reaches for. The silence may be… what $10 million a year sounds like.”

**Problem:** On p. 90 the investment is the cost of *actualizing* contact: “construct a great amount of equipment that would enable a ‘search for extraterrestrials’—and possibly also transmitting stations,” restated as “constructing a sizeable amount of specialized receiving equipment… an extremely costly one.” The 2026 cell pays this with HRMS / Breakthrough Listen *program* spend (~$10M/year). Those programs rent time on Green Bank, Parkes, MeerKAT and (via partnership) FAST — dishes built with public capital at hundreds of millions to billions. That is not the equipment bill he named.

The same essay then says the $10M search is too small to meet his p. 80 condition, so the null result cannot be scored. Those two verdicts cannot both be earned by the same figure. If $10M/year *is* the actualized search, the p. 80 row is not OPEN. If it is *not* the actualized search, the cost claim has not been tested and cannot be a MISS. “Never needed to” is the clause that is doing the work, and it is an inference, not a receipt.

**Fix:** Score the nuclear-comparison sentence only against the quantity he named (dedicated search-and-transmit capital, not an observing grant). If the complete search has not been built, the row is **OPEN** (or **EARLY** if SKA-class public arrays are the live path). If the claim is only that *society never spent* nuclear-research money on SETI, say that and do not also call $10M/year a serious search that “never needed” more. Do not let one number pay a MISS and an OPEN that deny each other.

### 2. “Every serious attempt to leave or to listen has been paid for by individuals” is false

**Location:** “What happened,” last paragraph: “Every serious attempt to leave or to listen has been paid for by individuals”; same claim in weaker form throughout: “The answer was none of them”; “the rulers never came.” Only `starshot-2016` marks the paragraph.

**Problem:** Ozma was NRAO. HRMS was NASA. Breakthrough Listen buys time on NSF/CSIRO/SARAO instruments and partners with China’s FAST, a state-built 500-metre dish that does SETI. NASA reopened technosignature proposals after a 2018 congressional direction. The ATA itself ran on NSF money before the 2011 collapse. The sentence as written is not a compression; it is wrong. It is also unmarked: `starshot-2016` does not cover listening.

A skeptic who knows FAST or Green Bank exists will stop at this sentence. The political story (US dedicated radio SETI is privately topped up) can be told without the universal.

**Fix:** Bound the claim to US *dedicated program* money after 1993, and say the dishes are public. Mention FAST (and SKA as a SETI science case) or drop “every” / “none of them.” Give the stronger sentence its own `<!-- CHECK -->` or cut it.

### 5. Note 3 and the 1974 update are unread

**Location:** whole essay; “Future Prospects” treated as 1964; “three sentences in a 1966 book”; no later-Lem.

**Problem:** AGENT: read the chapter endnotes; where they disagree with the body, the note is usually the better forecast. **Fix:** Quote note 3 and score isolation/camouflage as his selected hypothesis. Date Future Prospects as 1966/1974. Score 2026 against the later sentence, not only the 1964 frame.

**Fix:** Receipt the OPEN with a completeness/haystack paper, not with $10M/year. Restore “Today it is still too early.” If the chapter’s conclusion is hypothesis III / isolation (note 3), say so; do not invent a stronger conclusion to unsay.

### 9. Call-signs HIT grades a method the chapter already says Drake was using

**Location:** object row, efficiently coded message “starts to resemble noise” / “special ‘call signs’…” → **HIT**; “The information argument is now simply how the searches are built.”

**Problem:** The Shannon point (efficient code ≈ noise, so a beacon must waste bandwidth) is his, and it held. The 2026 cell then identifies that point with “narrowband, repetitive beacons,” which is how Ozma was already designed. p. 64: equipment “specially constructed for receiving ‘artificial’ signals, thus enabling their differentiation from ‘galaxy noise’,” searching for “some kind of regularity.” “Now simply how the searches are built” reads as a forecast fulfilled. It is a correct analysis that did not change practice.

The stronger claim — conversations “already today” in the archives — is hedged (“We cannot thus exclude”) and limited to “supercivilizations,” which the essay’s quote drops. Still open #3 handles that claim; the HIT should not swallow it.

**Fix:** Narrow the HIT to the encoding-looks-like-noise argument, restore “supercivilizations,” and stop saying this is why searches are built the way they already were in 1960. Or grade the method **HIT** as description, not as prediction.
