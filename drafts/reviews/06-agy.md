### 1. Misreading Lem: The Substrate of the "Human-Producing" Machine (Severity 1)
**Location:** `"It is incomparably more difficult to construct the second one [human-producing] than the first [travel]" (p. 206)` (Row 4) and prose: `The one that needs your whole nervous system is a hobby; the one that needs a keyboard is infrastructure.`
**Problem:** The essay fundamentally misreads Lem to manufacture a MISS. Lem was not comparing a VR headset to a text-based LLM. He explicitly defines both devices as *phantomatics* ("a 'travel' phantomat and a 'human-producing' one are two different kinds of devices"). His "human-producing" machine is the full nervous-system-intercepting VR *plus* an AI generating an interactive, intelligent persona inside it. By changing the substrate of the second machine to a "keyboard," the essay compares VR-without-AI to AI-without-VR, completely missing that Lem was comparing VR-without-AI to VR-*with*-AI. 
**Fix:** Grade Lem on what he actually wrote (an AI avatar inside fully immersive VR vs. a static VR scene). This would likely be an `EARLY` or `HIT`, and the essay should find a legitimate MISS elsewhere.

### 2. Missing MISS: The Impossibility of Shared Virtual Worlds (Severity 2)
**Location:** The essay quotes `a phantomatic world is a world of total solitude` (Row 11) to praise him for anticipating filter bubbles and personalized feeds.
**Problem:** The essay skips the very next sentence on page 207, where Lem gives his reasoning for this solitude: *"There cannot be more than one person in it at any one time, just as it is impossible for two real persons to find themselves in the same dream."* Lem explicitly predicted that multiplayer VR and shared virtual spaces were impossible. The essay suppresses this massive technological failure to praise his foresight instead.
**Fix:** Add an object-level ledger row quoting his claim that shared virtual worlds are impossible, and grade it a `MISS` (citing VRChat, Meta Horizon, or MMOs).

### 3. Factual Error in 2026 Claims: Chronology of VR Hardware (Severity 3)
**Location:** `When Lem revisited the book in 1982, Zylinska reports, he updated the chapter with the first commercial VR hardware — "virtual reality devices such as Eye Phone and Data Glove"`
**Problem:** This is chronologically impossible. Jaron Lanier's VPL Research didn't introduce the DataGlove and EyePhone until 1987 and 1989. Zylinska's introduction (p. 21) mentions both the 1982 essay ("Twenty Years Later") and the 1991 essay ("Thirty Years Later") before listing these devices. The essay incorrectly attributes the update to 1982, grading 1982-Lem on late-1980s technology.
**Fix:** Correct the date of the VR hardware update to 1991 (the "Thirty Years Later" essay).

### 4. Factual Error in 2026 Claims: Accessibility (Severity 4)
**Location:** `VR revenue is overwhelmingly games; surgical and flight simulation real but small; nothing for the blind <!-- CHECK: vr-use-mix -->` (Row 7)
**Problem:** The claim "nothing for the blind" is factually false. There is an established market of AR headsets and wearable devices (e.g., eSight, OrCam, and features in the Vision Pro) designed specifically to assist the visually impaired by reading text, recognizing faces, or enhancing contrast.
**Fix:** Remove "nothing for the blind" and replace it with a note that AR/headset accessibility tools do exist, which would score a `HIT` or `EARLY` for Lem's prediction.
