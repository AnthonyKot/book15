## Ranked findings

### 1. Both MISS rows are invalid as written — CONFIRMED (missed by both reviewers)

**Location:** ledger rows “The carriers … MISS” and “His bet against Ashby … MISS”; lede, “the machine does not exist and will not” and “The filter won.”

The polymer-carrier row does not meet §4’s definition of MISS: “did not happen **and there is no live path**.” Its only receipt is `funsearch-2023`, which proves that a digital competitor exists; it says nothing about the absence of a live molecular path. Lem also introduces synthetic polymers only “for example” (p.263), calls the farm “extremely difficult” rather than impossible, and on p.266 varies the possible substance and pseudo-organism form before conceding that the ideas cannot then be verified. Active molecular computation makes “will not” especially unsafe; even without adding new evidence, the registered receipt cannot pay that absence claim. Make the exact isodynamic molecular farm **OPEN**, unless a source can establish that its required mechanism has no live path. “Not built” alone is not MISS.

The Ashby row misstates both sides of the comparison. On p.261 Lem explicitly says that his position differs, but the distinction is **greatest, random/noise diversity** versus **significant, limited diversity that develops**. He does not reject filtering as such: his own farm repeatedly generates variants and passes them through “sieves.” FunSearch is not Ashby’s air-molecule generator either. It begins from a program skeleton, samples a structured LLM, preserves a diverse pool, and iteratively evolves high-scoring survivors; GNoME uses trained graph networks and active-learning filtration. Those receipts prove generate/evaluate loops, not that maximum random diversity beat structured development. Remove this MISS or rewrite the row around the precise p.261 distinction and grade it **OPEN** on current evidence.

These failures matter beyond two cells: the essay was pitched around two honest misses, but neither presently survives §4.

### 2. The essay’s central claim about selection reverses the corpus — CONFIRMED (missed by both reviewers)

**Location:** lede, “the part neither of them thought was hard”; “the part Lem treated as machinery — the sieve — is the part that broke”; ledger row “Selection, not production, is where the value enters.”

Ashby’s position on p.261 is that “the only things that really matter are elimination and selection.” Lem likewise spends pp.260–265 specifying an externally coupled selector and says on p.263 that setting up the farm is “extremely difficult.” The essay may fairly argue that neither anticipated **proxy validation** as the modern failure mode, but it cannot say that neither thought selection mattered or was hard. Rebuild the thesis narrowly: they foregrounded selection, but Lem’s proposed truth test assumed a reliable coupling to the phenomenon and did not analyze proxy failure.

The ledger row compounds the problem. “Selection, not production” belongs to Ashby’s p.261 position, while its quoted support—“that which expresses the environment most adequately survives” (p.259)—is Lem’s fitness rule, not a claim that production is trivial. Re-anchor and reword the row before deciding its verdict.

### 3. The 1964 baseline is partly false and mostly unreceipted — CONFIRMED; expands Agy 7

**Location:** “perceptrons that could not yet read, machines doing a million operations a second, and no automated experiment anywhere. `baseline-1964`”

The register row, filed under essay 4 rather than essay 7, receipts only the existence/dates of Beer, Ashby, Wiener, and Rosenblatt’s 1958 perceptron. ### 10. “Universal plagiarizer” is not a missing MISS — REJECTED (Agy 3)

P.241 does not claim that learning from or recombining a large corpus prevents creativity. It says that an **exact material copy containing all variables** ceases to be a model and cannot perform the variable selection that theory and creativity require. Generative models are aggressively lossy statistical abstractions, not exact copies of every variable in the phenomena they model. Agy’s proposed MISS reverses Lem’s argument; if graded at all, the modern analogy tends toward HIT or remains an ungraded conceptual parallel.
