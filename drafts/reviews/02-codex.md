# Consolidated review: Essay 2, “The last lawnmower”

The raw reports were both nonempty. I checked their claims against `CONTEXT.md`, `AGENT.md`, the HTML, `checks/claims-2026.tsv`, and corpus pages returned by `scripts/find.py`. Findings are ranked by effect on the argument, not by either reviewer's severity label.

## 1. The essay does not perform its assigned chapter-level job — CONFIRMED

**Reviewer:** Grok 6.

`CONTEXT.md` §6 requires both the bio/techno-evolution parallel **and** “Several Naïve Questions,” tested against designed versus evolved technology, scaling laws, emergent capabilities, and the unplanned stack. The essay stops at pp. 33–38 plus an endnote. It omits the whole required pp. 55–58 material, including Lem's refusal to reserve creative work for humans, his machines that “will not need programmers anymore,” and his distinction between being cleverer and being rebellious (both p. 57). It also omits the highly testable p. 43 claim that technology had produced only “narrowly specialized devices.”

The selected pitch, 2-A, explains the narrow angle but does not override the authority document; `CONTEXT.md` was never amended. This is a ship blocker independent of the ledger errors below.

**Required revision:** cover the contract material and add specific rows, or obtain and record an explicit contract amendment. The p. 43 specialization claim is a stronger candidate for an honest negative than forcing the hedged lawnmower example to carry one.

## 2. “The model has nowhere to put legislation” contradicts the chapter — CONFIRMED

**Reviewer:** Grok 1.

The essay says that the frame has “only one selection pressure,” that “everything in the chapter happens by competition in a niche,” and that it has no place for a legislature. Corpus p. 38 says the opposite at the level necessary to defeat those absolutes: technical evolution remains “in human hands,” is not “people-free,” and differs most from amoral bioevolution. Pages 47–49 then discuss moral choice and social systems; p. 51 says technoevolution can be accelerated or slowed through concentrated spending; pp. 56–57 explicitly imagine people refusing further automation.

Those passages do not give a developed theory of regulation as a selector, so the California/AM observation can survive as a useful 2026 specification. It cannot be presented as something the chapter has no conceptual place for.

**Required revision:** recast this as “Lem names human agency but does not model a legislature preserving an otherwise unfit technology,” and test that narrower claim at the problem level.

## 3. The supposed “Note 1” is note 6, is attached elsewhere, and does not recant the analogy — CONFIRMED

**Reviewer:** Grok 5.

Corpus pp. 355–356 identify the hybridization discussion as chapter **note 6**. The superscript is attached on p. 47 to “Everything else … is still ahead of us,” at the end of the theory-versus-empiricism discussion—not to the pp. 33–38 set piece. The note begins by saying a technical-evolution tree would “most likely resemble” a biological one and then introduces cross-fertilization as “the difficulty.” That qualifies the analogy; it does not “take a good deal of it back.” The chapter itself also immediately opens a section called “Differences” on p. 38, so “a disanalogy the chapter never mentions” is false.

This error appears in prose, the ledger, the Reading box, and the `CONTEXT.md` status entry.

**Required revision:** cite “note 6 (pp. 355–357), attached at p. 47,” describe it as a qualification, and remove every “Note 1” and “the chapter never mentions” formulation.

## 4. The radio HIT is paid by the wrong event — CONFIRMED

**Reviewers:** Grok 3; Agy 4.

Corpus p. 36 describes an already completed diversification under television pressure: pocket sets, specialized hi-fi sets, stereo, and integrated recorders “came into being.” The 2023 receipt describes manufacturers removing AM reception because of drivetrain interference. That is contraction or habitat loss, not “radiation of variations.” The legislation that follows may illustrate human selection, but it cannot pay this HIT.

Agy is also right that the essay shifts Lem's past-tense observation into the present-tense “answers.” Agy goes too far in calling the modern event a contradiction of radio diversification in general; it simply does not test it.

**Required revision:** cut the row or receipt continued diversification with an actual post-Lem sequence such as later radio formats/distribution niches. Keep AM only for the human-selection argument.

## 5. The omnibus morphological HIT launders untested and failed components — CONFIRMED

**Reviewer:** Grok 4.

The row claims one four-part pattern: copied/poor prototypes, radiation, gigantism before decline, and lateral relics. Its receipts test none of prototype copying or gigantism; the AM receipt mismatches radiation; the relic is separately OPEN; and EV continuity is used elsewhere to show Lem chose the wrong successor. The statement that the whole pattern “holds across the cases” therefore has no evidentiary basis. It violates AGENT's rule that “He got the idea” cannot launder “he got the thing wrong.”

**Required revision:** split the components. Score only those with direct receipts, leaving gigantism and relic formation OPEN or unscored.

## 6. The AlexNet row combines three claims but receipts at most one — CONFIRMED, with the reviewers’ rationale narrowed

**Reviewers:** Agy 2; Grok 10 (`alexnet-2012`).

The reviewers are right that the clean HIT is inflated, but Agy incorrectly reduces note 6 to the vague claim that unpredictable things happen. Lem gives a specific mechanism: cross-fertilization among technical domains, leading to acceleration and unpredictable turns. AlexNet's use of consumer GPUs is a plausible instance of hardware repurposing across domains. It does **not** establish “constant acceleration,” demonstrate that AlexNet was unforeseeable, or receipt the unmarked claim that deep learning is “the dominant technology of 2026.” One 2012 system cannot pay all three propositions.

Agy's proposed `RIGHT ANSWER, WRONG REASON` is also unsupported: the essay supplies no different wrong Lemian mechanism.

**Required revision:** narrow any HIT to cross-domain recombination and state that this is the essay's application. Receipt or cut the 2026 dominance claim; leave acceleration/unpredictability separate and OPEN if retained.

## 7. The air-cushion/lawnmower passage is scoreable, but the essay strips its hedges and invents a date — CONFIRMED, with the requested demotion REJECTED

**Reviewers:** Grok 2; Agy 3.

Corpus pp. 35–36 says the vehicles had not reached their late forms, then begins “One can also imagine”; the lawnmower follows under “it is quite likely that, say.” Lem supplies no date. Thus “rash enough to date it,” “the chapter's most datable prediction,” and the unhedged ledger cells misrepresent his confidence.

The reviewers overreach in saying this is merely rhetorical and cannot be graded. “Will make wheel drive obsolete” and “When the current automobile is eventually replaced” remain falsifiable future claims inside an expressly hypothetical scenario. A narrow MISS is defensible if the full hedge is restored and the essay stops treating it as a dated, hard forecast. The lawnmower row's current `OPEN` is more defensible than the surrounding prose.

**Required revision:** restore the governing language in the ledger, remove every claim that Lem dated it, and separate the air-cushion successor from the illustrative choice of a lawnmower.

## 8. The VTOL row grades a contemporary prototype as a forecast — CONFIRMED, but cutting all post-1964 testing is unnecessary

**Reviewers:** Agy 1; Grok 11; related to Grok 7.

Corpus p. 35 uses the present tense: the airplane “defends itself” by “creating a prototype.” The essay's own baseline has the P.1127 hovering in 1960. Calling this a “live prediction” and paying a HIT with the Harrier's 1969 entry into service inflates the distance Lem covered.

The reviewers' strongest remedy—remove it altogether—is unnecessary. Lem's actual mechanism is niche defense rather than helicopter displacement. Post-baseline persistence through the V-22 and F-35B can test that mechanism, but the row must say so and must not imply that Lem predicted civil eVTOL certification.

**Required revision:** make the already-existing prototype the baseline; make later persistence/non-displacement the test. Put civil eVTOL in separate prose or an `EARLY` row only if tied to a Lem claim.

## 10. The software conclusion is absolute and its quantitative receipt is weak — CONFIRMED; “illegitimate transposition” is REJECTED

## Findings both reviewers missed

### A. `RIGHT QUESTION, WRONG ANSWER` is the wrong verdict form for the software row — CONFIRMED

The p. 38 Lem cell is a declarative claim that the evolutions have similar dynamic laws, not a dilemma plus Lem's proposed resolution. The 2026 cell offers a counterexample. Under `CONTEXT.md` §4 this is not `RIGHT QUESTION, WRONG ANSWER`; after explicitly labelling software as the essay's test, it is either a narrow `MISS` or should be decomposed into a real problem-level question and answer.

### B. “The car was replaced” is false on the essay's own evidence — CONFIRMED

The first sentence of “What happened” says “The car was replaced.” The paragraph immediately says wheel drive, roads, and the car persist while the propulsion system changes. The intended claim is that the internal-combustion drivetrain is being displaced in new sales, not that the car was replaced.

### C. “Constant acceleration” is strengthened to “acceleration without limit” — CONFIRMED

The third Still Open item says note 6 “predicts acceleration without limit.” Corpus p. 356 says technoevolution's speed is “characterized by constant acceleration”; it does not add “without limit.” Elsewhere in the same chapter, p. 51 expressly discusses limits to technoevolution's speed. Preserve Lem's wording and test whether acceleration continued; do not insert an unbounded claim.

### D. The software section adds two unsupported absolutes beyond “no extinction” — CONFIRMED

“Nothing forces the switch” ignores forced migrations from regulation, vendor support, security, or hardware constraints, none of which the COBOL receipt tests. The Still Open phrase “something that costs nothing to keep running” is also unsupported and implausibly absolute; legacy operation plainly has costs even when replacement costs more. These claims need receipts or deletion.

### E. The claims register does not receipt its hovercraft baseline — CONFIRMED

`baseline-1964-ch2` names “cross-Channel hovercraft entering service,” but its source cell lists de Latil, Lévi-Strauss, NASA, and the P.1127 only. This is exactly the kind of marker/register mismatch `verify.sh` cannot catch: the ID exists, but one factual component has no source.

### F. The Senate cosponsor count is wrong, not merely stale — CONFIRMED

The essay says “more than sixty Senate cosponsors”; Congress.gov lists 60. Grok accepted that line while challenging the House number, so both reviewers missed the direct numerical error. Use “60 Senate cosponsors” with an as-of date, or drop both counts and say the bills attracted broad bipartisan sponsorship.
