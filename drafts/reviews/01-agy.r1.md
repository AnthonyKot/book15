**1. Misreading Lem & Verdict Inflation (Severe)**
* **Location:** `"Current estimates situate the production of the microfusion cell in the 1990s, or even later" (p. 28)`
* **Problem:** The essay claims "he does it himself" (makes a bad straight-line forecast), but Lem is explicitly reporting the consensus of others ("Current estimates situate..."). *AGENT.md* strictly dictates: "A thing being *reported* is not the thing being *true*." Furthermore, the essay grades it as a MISS for the 1990s, completely ignoring the explicit hedge "or even later" present in the very sentence it quotes, making the MISS unfair.
* **Fix:** Do not score a reported consensus estimate as Lem's own failed forecast. If scored, acknowledge the hedge "or even later" (which makes a MISS invalid) and evaluate it as OPEN or EARLY.

**2. The Two Layers & Verdict Inflation (Severe)**
* **Location:** Ledger rows 2, 3, and 4 ("Sudden turns", "Straight lines", "Closed schemas") under "Object level".
* **Problem:** Object-level and problem-level verdicts are hopelessly mixed. These three failure modes are abstract mechanisms of prediction failure (Problem level), yet they are listed as specific technologies (Object level). Additionally, the essay inflates verdicts by taking vague metatheoretical observations (e.g., surprises happen) and crediting them as specific HITs. Worse, it uses Lem's *own* failed forecasts (missing LLMs, missing EV wheels) to award him these HITs. 
* **Fix:** Move the three taxonomy modes to the "Problem level" section of the ledger. Grade them as problem-level insights rather than object-level HITs, and do not award HITs to Lem for his own missed forecasts.

**4. Unmarked and Overstated 2026 Claim (Moderate)**

* **Problem:** This factual claim about the present is missing a `<!-- CHECK: ... -->` marker. It is also factually challenged: Finland's Onkalo repository is a permanent deep geological repository for spent nuclear fuel built at scale and nearing operation.

**5. Unpersuasive to a Skeptical Reader (Moderate)**
* **Location:** The overall ledger structure grading Lem's taxonomy.
* **Problem:** A skeptic of the "Lem predicted everything" claim would not be persuaded by this ledger. Seeing vague methodological truisms graded as object-level "HITs"—especially when justified by Lem's *failures*—looks exactly like the rhetorical gymnastics and "prophet / not prophet" framing the book's rules explicitly forbid.
* **Fix:** Strip the rhetorical padding. Let his object-level misses stand independently, and argue clearly that his value lies in the problem-level taxonomy rather than object-level prescience.
