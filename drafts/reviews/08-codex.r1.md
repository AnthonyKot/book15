## Ranked findings

### 1. The essay misses the closest realization of Lem's actual machine

**CONFIRMED** from Grok 5, and more damaging than that report says.

The essay says the genotype level "did not arrive in the matchmaker" and that modern machines do not discourage matches (lines 41, 43, 56). That is false. Dor Yeshorim is a premarital genetic-compatibility programme: two prospective partners' identifiers are checked together, the result is compatible/incompatible, and its own instructions urge checking before the couple meets. This is very close to Lem's p. 345 second-stage process for rejecting pairings likely to produce offspring with serious disorders. See the programme's [process](https://doryeshorim.org/process/) and [FAQ](https://doryeshorim.org/faq/).

This evidence does not pay the whole p. 345 forecast. It covers the disease/disability branch, not selection for intelligence or personality; it operates in a bounded community; and it does not establish population-scale control of evolution. The ledger must therefore split Lem's own split:

- psychosomatic matchmaking: separately tested;
- partner-genotype compatibility for serious recessive disease: a bounded **HIT**;
- intelligence/personality selection: embryo products are a different mechanism and at most **EARLY** on the object;
- population evolution: **OPEN**.

The current thesis, "route right, device wrong," is consequently too neat. For one important branch, the device was substantially right.

### 2. The intelligence MISS is manufactured by changing both the comparator and the mechanism

**CONFIRMED** from Grok 2 and Agy 1.

On p. 346, "relatively easy" explicitly contrasts distributing high intelligence with "a deep transformation of man's systemic organization." On p. 347 the vehicle remains partner selection over potentially thousands of years. The essay instead tests one-generation selection among ten sibling embryos and calls the within-family limit Lem's MISS (lines 45, 57, 74). That is not his claim.

The receipt is also overstated. Karavani et al. report about 2.5 IQ points **given then-current predictors**, and state that expected gain rises with the square root of variance explained by the score; 2.5 is not a ceiling that "no better score removes." The finite within-family pool is a ceiling on embryo selection, but it does not test assortative mating across generations. See [Karavani et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6957074/).

Remove this MISS. If the essay wants to discuss embryo selection, label the transposition as the essay's and do not debit Lem for its within-family constraint. The clean negative available in his own forecast is the unfulfilled prescriptive/cultural-norm branch, though Dor Yeshorim means that claim must be bounded rather than universal.

### 3. The p. 347 problem-level HIT scores an outcome that the essay itself calls open

**CONFIRMED** from Grok 3 and Agy 6.

The row bundles two propositions: the difference in moral reaction, and the claim that surgery and matchmaking "can both lead to analogous results." Its receipt substitutes CRISPR and embryo screening for Lem's cutting-bodies/marriage-counselling poles, while Still open #3 correctly says convergence has not been demonstrated (lines 62, 82). A claim cannot be both **HIT** and **OPEN**.

Split the row. The visibility/disgust asymmetry may be a live and strong problem-level comparison, but "analogous results" remains **OPEN**. Embryo screening can illustrate a third, quieter reproductive path; it cannot silently become Lem's marriage machine.

The p. 344 "bottled fetuses" sentence should be restored because it frames this comparison, but Grok overreads it: Lem calls surgery and bottled fetuses science-fiction images and says the actual procedures **may** be invisible. He does not categorically predict that embryo procedures will never occur, and PGT-P is neither fetal development nor genetic engineering in the sense of editing a genome.

### 4. The adoption HIT combines a partial event, a failed conditional, and the wrong receipt

**CONFIRMED** from Grok 4 and Agy 5, with an additional receipt failure both missed.

At p. 345 Lem says there is "a significant chance" of wide adoption; only **when** the system becomes a cultural norm might a discouraged match become forbidden fruit. The row drops the first hedge, joins the conditional consequence to adoption, admits that consequence failed, and still awards **HIT** (line 55).

More basically, Rosenfeld et al. establish that meeting **online** became the most common meeting route for US heterosexual couples. They do not establish adoption or success of Lem's machine, which chooses a preselected group using researched psychosomatic compatibility. Online meeting is not evidence that an algorithm made the match. See [Rosenfeld, Thomas & Hausen 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1908630116).

The row needs at least two claims. Wide online meeting is a labelled cousin, not sufficient payment. A bounded matching-system claim needs a source about actual algorithmic recommendation. The prescriptive norm/forbidden-fruit branch needs its own verdict and must incorporate Dor Yeshorim rather than assert universal nonexistence.

### 5. The two-level **RIGHT ANSWER, WRONG REASON** row fails its own 2026 cell

**CONFIRMED** from Grok 6.

The Lem cell combines a two-stage partner selector, three ethically distinct trait classes, and population control. The 2026 cell says the mechanism is not partner matching and the outcome is "not evolution being controlled" (line 56). **RIGHT ANSWER, WRONG REASON** requires the answer/outcome to match; this row expressly says it did not.

Split it by object and scope. Dor Yeshorim pays the serious-disease partner-screening object. PGT-M and polygenic embryo products are related but different objects. The current Nucleus product menu does not establish control of evolution, and "item for item" is false because the essay adds appearance, which Lem did not list, while flattening his explicit moral distinction between disability ("correct") and intelligence/personality ("raises some doubts").

### 6. The millennial clock has not been falsified

**CONFIRMED** from Grok 7 and Agy 2.

P. 347 says marriage machines "could" take thousands of years to reach a desired species-level state. The essay answers that embryo selection "acts in one generation," while Still open #1 concedes that no population shift has been detected (lines 45, 58, 80). Producing a per-family selection differential in one generation is not demonstrating faster species-level change. The factual time forecast is therefore not a **RIGHT QUESTION, WRONG ANSWER** object-level row. With present evidence, it is **OPEN**; the unit-shift belongs in prose as the essay's comparison.

### 7. Several 2026 receipts are false, narrower than the prose, or cousins of the claim

**CONFIRMED** from Grok 8, Grok 9a/b/d/e/f, Agy 4, and Agy 8.

- **23andMe:** Regeneron won an auction bid but never closed. Bidding reopened and TTAM completed the acquisition on July 14, 2025. The data changed owner once, not twice. The register itself describes this correctly; the essay does not. See the [SEC completion filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1804591/000119312525158551/0001193125-25-158551-index.htm). More importantly, a database sale is a data-consent problem, not Lem's public authorization of a thousand-year hereditary programme. It cannot receipt that row as written.
- **Matching efficacy:** Joel et al. tested prediction of initial, relationship-specific desire at speed dates. They did not test whether algorithmic matching produces more stable relationships. Their negative result cannot pay "still no demonstration" or turn "better knowledge" into a MISS. See [Joel, Eastwick & Finkel 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797617714580).
- **He Jiankui:** he announced twins in 2018, but the later court judgment confirmed three gene-edited children. "No further ... children" is false. More broadly, a conviction and a professional call for a moratorium do not prove the research path "stopped." The societies [called for](https://www.isctglobal.org/telegrafthub/blogs/ken-ip1/2025/05/27/isct-arm-and-asgct-issue-unified-joint-statement-c) a moratorium; it is not an operative global ban. This is precisely the reported-versus-true distinction in `AGENT.md`.
- **Implanted insulin delivery:** ordinary contemporary insulin pumps are external continuous-subcutaneous systems. Fully implanted pumps exist but are rare, specialized devices; the register's claim that they are routine is not supported. Keep routine intrathecal pumps and subdermal implants, or source and qualify the insulin example.
- **Nucleus uncertainty:** "confidence intervals its buyers are not shown" has no check and is not established. Nucleus publicly discusses probabilistic uncertainty and publishes a gain calculator, even if that does not validate its claims. Cut the assertion or receipt a specific customer interface.
- **Population effect:** "actually changed the distribution of who gets born" conflicts with Still open #1 and lacks a marker. "Aims to change" is supportable; an observed population distribution shift is not.
- **Unchecked universals:** "No app discourages a marriage," "the machines do not give that kind of advice," and "attracts a fraction of the attention" are unmarked. The first two are contradicted at least in substance by Dor Yeshorim.

The launch-price complaint in Grok 9c is **REJECTED**: the essay explicitly dates the roughly $5,999 figure to June 2025, and contemporaneous coverage supports it. The current $9,999 price does not make the dated launch claim false.
