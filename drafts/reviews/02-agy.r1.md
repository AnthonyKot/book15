- **Problem:** Lem is describing an existing prototype in the present tense ("It does this by creating a prototype..."). As the essay's own `baseline-1964-ch2` check acknowledges, the first vectored-thrust jet (Hawker P.1127) was already hovering in 1960, four years before the book's publication. Scoring the description of an existing 1960s prototype as a successful 2026 forecast (HIT) is pure verdict inflation.

**3. Misreading Lem: Treating a hypothetical illustration as a hard prediction (Severity: High)**

- **Problem:** Lem's text says: "it is quite likely that, *say*, a small combustion-driven lawnmower will be the last descendant...". **4. Misreading Lem / Mixing Layers: The Radio Row (Severity: High)**
- **Location:** Ledger row 4 (Radio) and prose ("radio answers with 'a sudden "radiation of variations"...'").
- **Problem:** Lem was describing a *past* event—how the transistor allowed radio to escape TV by radiating into new forms (portable, car radios). The corpus text uses the past tense: "competition from television *led to* a sudden radiation." The essay obscures this by paraphrasing it into the present tense ("answers with") and grades it a HIT by citing a 2023 event where electric cars are dropping AM radio. This mixes a 1950s object-level description with a 2023 problem-level event. Furthermore, dropping a feature is habitat loss (extinction), not a "radiation of variations."
- **Fix:** Cut the row. Lem was describing the past, and the 2023 event contradicts the phenomenon he named.

**5. Overstated 2026 Claim: Software Extinction (Severity: Medium)**
- **Location:** Prose section "What happened" and Ledger row 7 ("Not for software, where there is no extinction").
- **Problem:** The essay points to the survival of COBOL to claim "software does not go extinct." This is a massive overstatement. While COBOL survives in core banking, thousands of software species, platforms, and languages (Flash, Silverlight, OS/2, etc.) are entirely extinct. Finding one survivor does not prove the absence of extinction.
- **Fix:** Soften the claim. State that "extinction in software is not guaranteed" or that "unlike biology, software relics can survive in the centre," rather than claiming absolute non-extinction.

**6. Misreading Lem: The Archaeopteryx Rule (Severity: Low)**
- **Location:** Prose section "Where he was worst" ("He applied that rule to the archaeopteryx and forgot it for the car.").
- **Problem:** The essay claims Lem applied the rule that "evolution is a tinkerer... and a lineage under pressure does not reinvent its body plan" to the archaeopteryx. He did not. He merely used the archaeopteryx to say prototypes are clumsy ("did not as much fly as flutter"). The "tinkerer" concept belongs to François Jacob (1977), not Lem.
- **Fix:** Cut the claim that Lem applied this specific rule to the archaeopteryx.
