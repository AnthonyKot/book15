### 4. The ledger violates the mandatory verdict and layer rules

**CONFIRMED** — Grok 5–7; Agy 4.

## Additional findings both reviewers missed

### A. The Goodhart sentence misattributes the familiar wording

The essay says “when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure” was stated by Goodhart in 1975. The claims register correctly records that this formulation is Strathern’s 1997 wording; her paper prints it directly and then relates it to Goodhart’s earlier monetary-policy observation. [Strathern, 1997](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1997-strathern.pdf)

This is a factual error, not merely a citation preference.

### B. The essay contradicts itself about human brakes and benefit rules

Facebook’s “own researchers” found the problem and proposed fixes ([line 39](/home/diablo/book15/chapters/04-the-regulator-that-games-its-goal.html:39)), yet the ledger and “Where he was worst” say there was no advisory body or brake until journalists appeared.

Likewise, the essay presents Robodebt as issuing automated welfare debts, then says no state has delegated “a benefit rule to a model outright” ([line 76](/home/diablo/book15/chapters/04-the-regulator-that-games-its-goal.html:76)). Either rules-based Robodebt counts, contradicting the open claim, or it does not count as a Lem-like sub-ruler.

### C. AI debate is evidence of a proposed remedy, not Lem’s coin toss

The `ai-debate` receipt establishes that machines arguing before a human judge is a live proposal. It does not establish Lem’s specific result: that the human cannot adjudicate disagreement and is reduced to chance. That claim remains `OPEN`; the current receipt belongs in explanatory prose, not as evidence that the dilemma has already materialized.

### D. “Markets did the regulating” is an unsupported false binary

The sentence contrasts an absent state machine with markets as though economic regulation passed wholly to one side. Modern economies remain extensively regulated by governments, central banks, administrative systems, and firms. Narrow the claim to where the relevant opaque optimisers were deployed: predominantly inside firms, not “markets” in general.
