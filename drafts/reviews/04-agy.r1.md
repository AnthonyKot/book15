### 1. Misreading Lem
- **Location**: "A Black Box 'on the throne of economic power' regulating a national economy (p. 117)" and "He put the machine in the wrong building... The Regulator of the Highest Kind... was the natural 1964 shape for a machine that governs, and it is the shape that did not happen."
- **Problem**: The essay grades Lem on the "Regulator of the Highest Kind" installed by the state as if it were his *prediction*. It wasn't. Lem introduces it (p. 117) with "Let us imagine its aim is..." explicitly to perform a thought experiment. He concludes the thought experiment three pages later by stating: "We have thus conducted a *reductio ad absurdum* of the theory of the Black Box: The Regulator of the Highest Kind" (p. 120), arguing that such a ruling machine is "undesirable." The essay grades him for a proposal he was explicitly refuting. Furthermore, the essay claims he put it in the state instead of private firms, but his very first example on p. 115 is a private firm (Beer's steel mill).
- **Fix**: Remove the claim that he placed it in the "wrong building." Treat the state regulator section as a thought experiment on the dangers of cryptocracy, not a failed prophecy. If grading his actual predictions of economic governance, use his later passage on "Coordinators" (pp. 158–160). 

### 2. Missing MISS
- **Location**: The ledger lacks a MISS for the substrate of intelligence.
- **Problem**: `CONTEXT.md` §6 explicitly instructs that for Essay 4, "the ledger will show what he did *not* foresee (...language as the substrate)." While the essay successfully notes that he foresaw learning from data (statistical correlations) rather than design, it completely skips the instruction to dock him for failing to foresee that *language* (LLMs) would be the substrate and scale the method for these black boxes, rather than cybernetic sociology or homeostats. 
- **Fix**: Add a `MISS` row to the ledger pointing out that Lem envisioned cybernetic systems and homeostats, completely missing that the first general-purpose "black boxes" would be built entirely out of language modeling.

### 3. Verdict Inflation
- **Location**: Ledger row: `Rival homeostats in a "struggle for life," one "bankrupting all its competitors" by doing its task too well (p. 116)` graded as a `HIT (in the pricing form; no economy-wide collapse)`.
- **Problem**: This is a forced HIT that flips Lem's meaning. Lem described autonomous AIs destroying the economy through hyper-competitiveness and bankrupting each other. The essay maps this to the DOJ v. RealPage lawsuit, which involves a *single* centralized software tool used by landlords to *collude* and fix prices (avoiding competition). A cartel is the exact opposite of rival homeostats bankrupting each other.
- **Fix**: Grade this as a `MISS` (we don't have rival autonomous AIs bankrupting each other) or `RIGHT QUESTION, WRONG ANSWER`.
- **Location**: Ledger row: `A Black Box "on the throne of economic power" regulating a national economy (p. 117)` graded as a `MISS`.
- **Problem**: As noted above, grading a *reductio ad absurdum* thought experiment as a failed prediction is an unfair MISS.
- **Fix**: Remove this row.

### 4. The Two Layers
- **Location**: Problem-level ledger row 4 (`HIT (diagnosis); OPEN (remedy)`) and row 6 (`RIGHT QUESTION, PARTLY WRONG ANSWER`).
- **Problem**: `CONTEXT.md` §4 specifies a hard rule: "every ledger row uses exactly one" verdict from the provided vocabulary. The essay violates this by using two verdicts in one row and inventing a new verdict ("PARTLY WRONG ANSWER").
- **Fix**: Split row 4 into two separate rows (one for the diagnosis, one for the remedy). Change row 6 to a strict `RIGHT QUESTION, WRONG ANSWER` (or just `RIGHT QUESTION`), and move the nuance into the prose.

### 5. 2026 Claims
- **Location**: "his rival homeostats colluding without a meeting arrived as algorithmic pricing: the U.S. Department of Justice sued RealPage in 2024..."
- **Problem**: Overstated and factually flawed framing. RealPage's YieldStar is a centralized platform that landlords explicitly feed their private data into. It is very much a "meeting" of data in a central hub, not independent AI agents spontaneously learning to collude without interacting.
- **Fix**: Clarify that RealPage is a centralized platform, which makes it a poor fit for Lem's "rival homeostats," or find an actual example of independent algorithmic pricing bots learning to collude (which exists in academic literature).

### 6. Reader
- **Location**: The overall persuasiveness of the essay (ledger vs. prose).
- **Problem**: A skeptic of the "Lem predicted everything" claim would likely find the ledger unconvincing on its own. The ledger stretches definitions to force HITs (equating Amazon's rigid productivity quotas to a cybernetic homeostat making "moral decisions"; equating RealPage to "rival homeostats"). The skeptic would see that the essay's *rhetoric* is doing all the heavy lifting—using clever metaphors ("The steel mill's descendant is the recommender system") to map 1964 cybernetic theory onto 2024 tech. Without the prose holding it together, the ledger looks like classic prophecy-matching (finding vague similarities and claiming a hit).
- **Fix**: Make the object-level verdicts stricter. Acknowledge that the specific cybernetic contraptions Lem described did *not* come to pass, and lean heavier on the problem-level HITs where Lem's philosophical foresight truly shines.
