### 1. p. 243 device HIT is inflated, and the quote is cropped to make it so

**Location:** ledger row 1; also “The device on p. 243 got built, at a smaller scope than the sentence implies.”

**Problem:** The full passage is: “We are to invent a device that will gather information, generalize it **in the same way the scientist does**, and present the results of this inquiry to experts. … Our device thus **produces theories**.” A theory, two sentences later, is “a system that consists of symbols,” a structural equivalent that predicts successive states. The ledger replaces “in the same way the scientist does” with an ellipsis and never quotes “produces theories.”

The receipts do not meet “substantially as described”:
- Adam (2009) proposed and tested functions for orphan yeast genes — a bounded experimental loop, not a theory of a class of phenomena.
- Coscientist optimized known palladium-catalysed couplings.
- The AI Scientist produced one of three submissions that cleared an ICLR 2025 *workshop* review (ICBINB), then was withdrawn by prior agreement and never published. The *Nature* 2026 paper (Lu et al.) is a human-authored description of the system, not the machine paper.

**Fix:** Restore the cropped clause and the “produces theories” sentence. Regrade **EARLY** at demonstrated lab-task scope, or keep HIT only if the 2026 cell is narrowed to Adam’s gather–hypothesize–test loop and the other two are demoted to prose. State that the workshop paper was withdrawn and that *Nature* published the human write-up.

### 2. Third-language EARLY credits Lem with the thing essay 4 recorded as an omission, and misreads “causal”

**Location:** “a competence grown outside a brain, from written language, now acts on the world through tools”; ledger: “crossing the threshold of ‘understanding’ and acting causally (pp. 277, 280)” → EARLY.

**Problem:** p. 280 is not “train a statistical model on the existing comprehending language.” It is: natural language should develop, via tools autonomized in extracerebral systems, “a causal language ‘of the next kind’” which “almost by accident, will cross the threshold of ‘understanding.’” Causal, on p. 273, means “word literally becomes flesh” — the utterance *is* the construction (heredity). Tool-calling LLMs are still comprehending language that then triggers a separate action (closer to someone shouting “Down!” on p. 267 than to a gene).

p. 277 is a different claim and points the other way: “causal language will **stop being** comprehending, while science will be producing predictions stripped of ‘explanations.’” The essay glues 277 and 280 with “and by which,” as if one forecast.

Giving EARLY for “competence grown from written text” launders essay 4’s recorded omission (training on text at scale as the route to competence) into a hit for this chapter.

**Fix:** Split the claims. p. 280 as a new *causal* language → OPEN or EARLY only against DNA/protein language models or constructors, not GPT-3 + tools. p. 277 (predictions without explanations / control without understanding) is the chapter’s sharpest testable sentence and is not in the ledger — add it as its own row. Keep the text-training route in prose as the essay-4 omission, not as Lem’s third language arriving.

### 3. Sieve HIT treats FunSearch as wired to “the world,” and contradicts the essay’s own “worst” section

**Location:** “Where the evaluator runs the world, results hold (FunSearch)”; ledger problem-level HIT on a sieve “connected… to a real phenomenon” (p. 264).

**Problem:** FunSearch’s evaluator runs a *program* (cap set, bin packing) and counts. That is a formal oracle, not Lem’s sieve, which encodes “structural information about the phenomenon’s state” from a real external process into molecular specks and precipitates matches. The “worst” section then says the thing he missed is that cheap-to-evaluate problems and important ones are different sets, and “a crystal that has to be synthesised… is not a bin-packing heuristic you can score in a millisecond.” The ledger uses FunSearch as the positive case of his rule; the prose uses it as the counterexample to the rule’s reach.

A-Lab *was* coupled to a real phenomenon (synthesis, diffraction) and failed on the *encoding* of that phenomenon’s state — the interesting case, and closer to his apparatus than FunSearch is.

The row also mixes layers: the quoted sieve is an object-level mechanism; the graded claim is a problem-level design rule.

**Fix:** Do not call a formal evaluator “the world.” Either (a) problem-level HIT/OPEN on the *rule* that a selector coupled only to a proxy fails, paid by A-Lab / GNoME / the workshop, with FunSearch labelled as cheap formal evaluation; or (b) keep p. 264 as object-level and do not give HIT for a molecular, isodynamic, speck-precipitating sieve. Do not use FunSearch as both the confirmation and the exception.

### 6. Combinatorial-wall HIT is a tautology paid by the wrong domain

**Location:** “Holds as written, and the escape clause describes the world: image datasets sit on manifolds two to three orders of magnitude below their pixel count” → HIT.

**Problem:** Lem’s sentence is a conditional about *scientific* variables “as long as we cannot see any link between them.” Pope et al. (ICLR 2021) measure intrinsic dimension of MNIST/CIFAR/ImageNet (ImageNet: 150,528 pixels, ID ~26–43). That is representation learning on pixels, not hypothesis search over unlinked experimental variables. “Nobody enumerates a few dozen genuinely independent variables, then or now” and “systems that handle millions do it because the variables are not independent” are present-day claims with no marker except `intrinsic-dimension-2021`, which does not pay them.

Confirming “if unlinked, search explodes; real images are linked” is confirming a near-tautology plus a fact about photographs. That is not HIT on the p. 239 scientific ceiling.

**Fix:** Either OPEN (2026 has no receipt on independent *scientific* variables), or a much narrower row: exhaustive search of unlinked high-cardinality variables is still impossible; do not pay it with image manifolds. Receipt or cut “nobody enumerates…”.
