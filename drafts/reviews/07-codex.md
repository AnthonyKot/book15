## 1. Lem's note 8 supplies the chapter's missing second sieve and reverses the last OPEN row — MISSED BY BOTH

**Location:** the whole essay, especially “the sieve has to be wired to the phenomenon,” the problem-level row “Automating production as the answer to the information deluge,” and the retraction/paper-mill discussion.

**Finding:** On pp. 365–367, Lem explicitly says an information farm may “exacerbate, rather than diminish” the information crisis; cannot decide what a civilization ought to do; may become a “gigabyte” bomb; and, without an effective “addressing sieve,” will produce a paper deluge that makes further research impossible. He even forecasts insignificant papers produced for degrees or ambition and says automated librarianship and publishing selection are more urgent than open-ended cognition. The essay uses the 2023 retraction record as evidence that Lem's remedy remains untested while omitting a passage in which Lem predicts substantially that failure mode.

This is not a side note. It distinguishes two selectors:

- a **truth sieve**, coupled to the phenomenon (pp. 259, 264); and
- an **addressing sieve**, coupled to the civilization's questions and capacity to use the answer (pp. 365–367).

The essay's declared “one rule” is therefore only half of Lem's rule. It also makes “he never asks what happens when … a proxy gets substituted” too broad: he does not discuss slow or costly experiments, but he does discuss selection becoming detached from human relevance and drowning in its own output.

**Fix:** Bring note 8 into “What Lem said,” split the sieve analysis into truth and addressing, and add a problem-level row for the forecast that automated knowledge production without addressing will worsen the deluge. Rebuild the final OPEN row after that; pp. 281 and 237–239 do not support its current wording.

## 2. The A-Lab evidence is obsolete and now cuts against the essay's proxy-failure story — G9 CONFIRMED

**Location:** “41 novel compounds from 58 targets”; “outside crystallographers … concluded that no new materials had been made”; ledger row on the selector.

**Finding:** The current Nature record no longer supports those numbers or that formulation. The January 2026 author correction says novelty meant new to the prediction platform, not necessarily new to science, and reports post-publication confirmation of 36 of 40 claimed successes; the corrected paper now reports 36 compounds from 57 targets. The essay and `checks/claims-2026.tsv` still carry 41/58 and treat the 2024 critique as though it established failure to synthesize rather than a dispute over novelty and phase identification. [Nature's correction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09992-y.pdf) and [corrected article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06734-w) supersede the receipt.

This matters to the argument, not just the arithmetic: a 36/40 post-publication confirmation does not cleanly demonstrate that a proxy selector “wobbled.”

**Fix:** Update the prose and claim rows to the corrected 36/57 and 36/40 figures; distinguish “new to the platform” from “new to science”; state exactly which four results remained inconclusive and what the external critique still disputes. Reassess whether A-Lab remains a negative example in the sieve row.

## 3. The p. 243 functional HIT is inflated by an ellipsis — G1 CONFIRMED; A2 REJECTED

**Location:** first object-level row, “A device that will gather information, generalize it…”.

**Finding:** The corpus gives the load-bearing clause as “generalize it **in the same way the scientist does**” and immediately adds “Our device thus **produces theories**” (p. 243). The ledger's ellipsis removes the first clause and its evidence never confronts the second. Adam generated and tested bounded gene-function hypotheses; Coscientist planned and optimized reactions; the AI Scientist performed computational ML research. These establish increasingly complete scientific loops, but not plainly Lem's device producing theories in the full sense he defines on pp. 243–244. “At a smaller scope” in the prose does not narrow the ledger claim.

G1 is therefore confirmed: the full sentence is not paid by a clean HIT. A2's proposed **RIGHT ANSWER, WRONG REASON** is rejected, however. The ledger deliberately scores the functional specification separately from the substrate bet. Importing the mechanism into this row would make it compound and double-score p. 251. The right correction is to narrow the claim to a demonstrated gather–hypothesize–test loop or grade the full claim **EARLY**.

The AI Scientist did undergo real human workshop review; it was not merely scored by its own automated reviewer. One of three submissions cleared the workshop threshold and was withdrawn under a pre-agreed protocol. The essay should say that Nature published the human-authored system paper, not the generated workshop paper. [Lu et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10265-5)

**Fix:** Restore the omitted words and “produces theories.” Either narrow the 2026 side and keep HIT only for the bounded loop, or retain Lem's full specification and use EARLY. Clarify the withdrawal and the identity of the Nature paper.

## 4. The third-language row splices two different forecasts and misdefines “causal” — G2 and G11 CONFIRMED

**Location:** “a competence grown outside a brain, from written language, now acts on the world through tools”; fourth object row, pp. 277 and 280.

**Finding:** In Lem's scale, ordinary speech already has both causal and comprehending aspects (pp. 267–268). Purely causal language is hereditary language in which the utterance itself materializes its content—“word literally becomes flesh” (pp. 273–274). An LLM emitting text that a separate tool runner executes is not thereby the “causal language of the next kind” forecast on p. 280.

The essay also joins different claims. Page 277 forecasts a bifurcation in which causal language **stops being comprehending** and science produces predictions without explanations. Page 280 forecasts a richer causal language that, after natural language develops autonomous extracerebral tools, “almost by accident” crosses a threshold of understanding. The current sentence makes p. 277 sound like the output of p. 280 and then pays both with GPT-3 plus tool use. It also turns essay 4's recorded omission—training on text at scale as the route to competence—into a credit for Lem here.

G11 is confirmed as a separate ledger omission: pp. 276–277 contain the chapter's direct, testable forecast that control will outrun understanding and predictions will become detached from explanations, but the ledger never scores it on its own.

**Fix:** Split p. 277 from p. 280. Score control/prediction without explanation against appropriate scientific systems. Leave the p. 280 next-kind causal language OPEN or EARLY only with evidence that matches Lem's materializing sense of causality. Keep text-at-scale as the omission recorded in essay 4.

## 5. FunSearch is not a sieve “connected … to a real phenomenon” — G3 CONFIRMED

**Location:** “Where the evaluator runs the world, results hold (FunSearch)” and the problem-level sieve HIT.

**Finding:** Lem's sieve receives structural information from an external phenomenon and molecularly compares a carrier's predicted state with that phenomenon's actual state (pp. 259–260, 263–264). FunSearch executes a program against a formal evaluator for cap sets or bin packing. Its score is determinate, but it is not “the world” in Lem's stated sense. The essay then correctly uses FunSearch as the easy, cheap-to-evaluate contrast to physical materials work, contradicting the ledger's use of it as the positive empirical case.

The row is legitimately problem-level if it is explicitly framed as a general design rule; G3 overstates the layer objection on that point. Its main objection is nevertheless confirmed because the evidence does not instantiate the quoted rule.

**Fix:** Use a genuinely empirical closed loop for the world-coupled case and treat FunSearch separately as formal search with an exact evaluator. Reassess A-Lab after the 2026 correction rather than using it as the automatic counterexample.

## 6. The p. 251 row is compound and misplaced, but the reviewers' proposed MISS is not in Lem — G5 and A4 CONFIRMED; G4 and A3 REJECTED

## 7. The combinatorial-wall HIT is paid by evidence from the wrong domain — G6 CONFIRMED

**Location:** first problem-level row; “Nobody enumerates a few dozen genuinely independent variables.”

**Finding:** Lem's claim concerns hypothesis formation over variables whose relations are not known (p. 239). Pope et al. measures the intrinsic dimension of image datasets. Showing that natural images occupy a low-dimensional manifold does not show that no system can handle more than several dozen independent scientific variables, nor does it test Lem's numerical ceiling. The current HIT reduces to the truism that exhaustive search grows combinatorially and then illustrates it with a different task.

The two present-day universals—“Nobody enumerates…” and systems handle millions only because variables are dependent—are not separately receipted.

**Fix:** Narrow the row to the combinatorics of exhaustive enumeration and source that exact claim, or grade Lem's scientific-variable ceiling OPEN. Do not use image manifolds as direct confirmation.
