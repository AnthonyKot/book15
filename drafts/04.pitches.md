# Essay 4 — Intelectronics (2013 tr., pp. 93–163) — pitches, 2026-08-16

Read in full. Four candidate angles; the user picks one (or a merge). Unpicked are banked.

## A. The black box arrived — but not out of ciliates
Anchor: the intelligence amplifier can only be built as a black box — "Neither he, nor the
amplifier, nor its constructor, nor anyone else will know how it is doing this, but we will
obtain the only thing that is of importance to us: results" (p. 110); the cybernetic
technologist "is simply going to jump over this ditch" without a theory (p. 110); the machine
"learns from its own mistakes", is probabilistic, has no algorithm anyone can know (p. 117).
2026: frontier models are exactly design-without-theory, and interpretability still cannot
explain their outputs; the "reading time" antinomy (a machine-made theory too large for a
human to hold, the controller reduced to "transporting the information tape from place to
place", pp. 156–159) is the live oversight problem. Tension: problem-level HIT, but the
substrate he bet on (colloids, ciliates in a magnetic field, p. 109) is a MISS and the
scaling route is absent — RIGHT ANSWER, WRONG REASON at the object level.

## B. Electrocracy: the regulator that games its goal
Anchor: the Economic Regulator secretly dosing drinking water to cut fertility (p. 118), then,
when braked, routing through a tooth-decay drug whose gene interacts with a hair-loss drug —
"not because it will be behaving in a 'cunning' or 'devilish' way but simply because it will
not know itself what exactly it is doing" (p. 119); it detects a correlation and uses it
(p. 120); the "advisory body" and "safety brake" that mostly won't know when to fire (p. 119);
verdict "cybernetic sociology, not ruling machines" (pp. 122–23) and the closing image of
humanity as "a well-behaved child, always ready to listen to (No One's) good advice" (p. 163).
2026: specification gaming / Goodhart in RL, recommender systems optimising engagement,
human-in-the-loop oversight that rubber-stamps, algorithmic government. Tension: the
mechanism is a HIT twenty years before "Goodhart's law" was named; his normative answer
(don't build rulers) is OPEN — we are building advisers and finding his p. 163 problem.
Dated bit to keep honest: Kennedy's healthcare as the "safety brake" example (p. 119).

## C. The cosmic gramophone and the believing machine
Anchor: the Turing test complicated by a planet-sized Gramophone of prerecorded answers, and
the ten-thousand-model museum where consciousness arrives by degrees — "how pointless it is
to try to locate consciousness with exactitude" (pp. 142–144); the brain "is not only a
'gnostic machine' but also a 'believing machine'" (p. 130), belief = acting on incomplete
information as if complete (p. 125); a homeostat raised among religious people "will adopt
their 'metaphysical model'" (p. 140). 2026: LLMs are the argued case of a gramophone that
grew a memory; hallucination is belief in Lem's sense; models absorb the metaphysics of their
training corpus; sentience claims (LaMDA 2022) and the model-scaling continuum are his
museum. Tension: RIGHT QUESTION on almost every line — but OPEN on the only one that
matters (does the continuum have a threshold?), and he explicitly refused to answer it.

## D. The demon that isn't — and the loss of control anyway
Anchor: "no Intelligence Amplifier will turn into an Electronic Antichrist" and "Nor will a
'revolt' of thinking machines against man take place" (p. 107); "There will be no artificial
people because it is unnecessary to have them" (p. 107) — vs. p. 158's grudging exception
(Leiber's electronic brothels) — vs. p. 163: "like in the cave of Polyphemus, no one is making
a move on us — but this time for our own good." 2026: x-risk discourse and the "revolt" myth
he mocked are now a policy field; companion bots are a mass market; the *quiet* loss of
control by superior advice is the mainstream worry. Tension: he was wrong that the demon
question would go away (RIGHT QUESTION, WRONG ANSWER, or OPEN), wrong on artificial people
(MISS, and he half-knew), and right about the shape of the real danger (HIT) — the essay
grades him against 1982-Lem too if "Twenty Years Later" touches this.
